Leave the light on

One of our four values is “Leave the light on”: be kind, especially at 2 a.m. Mira Castell on the night it came from, what it asks of us and what it doesn’t.

MC

Mira CastellCEO & co-founder, Lisbon

24 Nov 2026·4 min read·Filed in Spark

In March 2022, at 1:52 in the morning Lisbon time, a musician in Toronto sent us a message. Her first album was due to go out at midnight, her time, and the masters she had uploaded to her lane were playing back as silence. She had two hours, a listening party in her living room and a lot of friends on the way.

There were three of us then, all in Lisbon, all asleep. We found the message at half past eight. The fix took twelve minutes. Her album went out almost five hours late, and she was kinder about it than she had any reason to be.

The bug was ours. The silence afterwards was worse. That night is where the fourth of our values comes from, the one people ask about most: Leave the light on.

What it asks of us

The short version is painted on the wall of the Lisbon hub: be kind, especially at 2 a.m. Launch nights run long, and somebody is always further from home than you. Answer the message.

The people who use Crestora make things at strange hours. The voice memo at one in the morning, the render that finishes at four, the launch that has to go out at midnight somewhere it is the middle of our night. If we build the road for that, somebody has to be on it.

In practice it means three small promises. Every message gets a human reply, not a ticket number. Every launch booked in Countdown has a named person from our crew on call for its first hour. And nobody who writes to us at night is made to feel that they shouldn’t have.

What it doesn’t ask

It doesn’t ask anybody at Crestora to be awake at 2 a.m. That was the first thing we got wrong. In 2023, for three months, two of us slept with a phone on the pillow during launch weeks. It made us tired, then short with each other, then short with the people we were trying to help, which is exactly the opposite of the point.

What fixed it was a map. We opened a hub in Toronto in 2024, in Seoul in 2025 and in Nairobi this year. Across four cities it is always somebody’s working day, so the night desk moves west with the evening: Seoul hands it to Nairobi, Nairobi to Lisbon, Lisbon to Toronto, and Toronto back to Seoul. A message sent at 2 a.m. in Lisbon is answered in Seoul over a late-morning coffee. The median reply at night is now six minutes, and nobody is typing it in pyjamas.

Kindness at 2 a.m. is short

We write a lot of messages to tired people. Over the years we have collected a few rules for them, and they have crept into how we write the product too:

  • The fix first, the explanation after. Nobody wants the history of a bug at 2 a.m.
  • One question at a time. If you need three answers, ask for the one that unblocks the others.
  • Never write “just”. Nothing is just anything at 2 a.m.
  • Say when you’ll be back, and be back by then.
  • If it was our fault, say so in the first sentence.

The light, on the inside

The value is about how we treat each other as much as the people we work for. Launch days are our busiest, and busy days are when people are least kind without meaning to be. So we try to make kindness the easy option. Countdown’s launch rooms have a quiet mode that holds anything non-urgent until morning. The night desk rota can be swapped without giving a reason. Anyone who works past midnight on a launch takes the next morning off, with no form and no asking. And when something does go wrong overnight, the person who fixed it writes the morning note for everyone else: what happened, what we changed, and who to thank.

And we answer the message. Even when it says “is it meant to look like this?” at 3:40 in the morning. Especially then.

Four years later

The musician from Toronto still rides with us. Her third album came out in October, at midnight her time. I was asleep in Lisbon; the Seoul desk was halfway through lunch. Everything went fine. The only message we got from her that night said: “Thanks for leaving the light on.”

If you’d like to work on a team that keeps it on, we’re hiring in all four hubs.
