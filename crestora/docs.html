Getting started

A lane is one project, from the first idea to the day it goes out. Opening your first one takes about two minutes, and the Solo plan gives you three active lanes for free.

  1. 01

    Open a lane. Choose Open a lane, give it a working title and say what you’re making: a film, an app, a drop, a podcast. The title can change later; the lane keeps its history either way.

  2. 02

    Drop in your sparks. Paste links, drag in sketches or forward an email to the board’s own address. Everything lands on the lane’s Sparkboard, at stage 01.

  3. 03

    Shape a one-page plan. Pick a Blueprint template, say what done looks like and set a launch day. Milestones fill in backwards from that date.

  4. 04

    Invite your crew. Add the people making it with you, and send a review link to anyone who only needs to leave notes. People with a review link never need an account.

Moving in from somewhere else? Bring folders, spreadsheets and past work with you. See Importing.

Lanes

Every project in Crestora rides one lane, and every lane moves through the same six stages. The stage decides which tool opens first and where the lane sits on your board, but nothing is locked away: files, notes and plans stay on the lane wherever it is.

Stages

  1. 01SparkSparkboard
  2. 02ShapeBlueprints
  3. 03MakeWorkbench
  4. 04ReviewReview Room
  5. 05LaunchCountdown
  6. 06AfterglowAfterglow

A colour always means its stage, here and in the app: a blue dot is still a spark, an amber one is waiting on feedback, a pink one has launched and is gathering its numbers. The full spectrum only ever means the whole flow.

Moving a lane

Drag the lane’s marker along its track, or press ] and [ to step it forward and back. Moving is never blocked but for one rule: a lane can’t enter Launch while one of its versions is still waiting for approval, unless an Owner overrides it.

Every move goes into the lane’s history with who moved it and when, and your crew gets one quiet notification, not one per file.

Merging lanes

Two lanes that turn out to be one project can merge. Choose Merge into… from the lane menu and pick the lane that stays. Versions, notes and sparks move across with their original dates, and the merged lane keeps the later stage of the two.

A merge can be undone from the lane’s history for 30 days. Merging arrived in v4.10.

Stage 01 · Spark

Sparkboard

Sparkboard is where ideas land before they’re ready to be anything. Voice memos, screenshots, links and 2 a.m. sketches arrive on one board, and Crestora groups them so the good ones find each other.

Capture shortcuts

The fastest spark is the one you don’t have to open an app for. Capture works from wherever the idea finds you:

Where you areHow to capture
At your desk⌘ ⇧ Space opens Quick capture over any app (Ctrl Shift Space on Windows and Linux).
In a browserThe Crestora button in your browser’s toolbar, or right-click anything and choose Send to Sparkboard.
On your phoneShare to Crestora from any app, or press and hold the app icon to record a voice memo. Capture works offline on iOS and syncs when you’re back.
In your inboxForward anything to the board’s address, shown under Board settings, such as nightbloom.k7@spark.crestora.example.

Constellations

Crestora reads what each spark is about and links it to sparks that share a subject, a palette or a reference. Linked sparks form a constellation, which you can name, pin or pull apart. Constellations are only ever suggestions: nothing is moved or deleted without you.

When one is ready, choose Promote to lane. Its sparks become the new lane’s starting board, and the lane opens at Shape.

Grouping runs on Crestora’s own servers, and your sparks are never used to train models. More in Security.

Stage 02 · Shape

Blueprints

A Blueprint is a one-page plan: what it is, who’s riding along, what done looks like and when the lights go green. It’s the page you send when someone asks what you’re working on.

Templates

Start from a blank page or from one of 40 templates written by working creators. Each template is a set of sections and a milestone pattern, so a short film starts with a shot list and a festival deadline, and an app starts with its scope and a beta date.

  • 9Film & animation
  • 8Apps & games
  • 7Music & audio
  • 6Writing & publishing
  • 6Objects & drops
  • 4Events & suppers

Milestones

Set a launch day and Blueprints works backwards: milestones land on your calendar with sensible gaps between them, skipping weekends if you ask it to. Drag one and the ones after it move along. Milestones sync both ways with calendars you’ve connected, such as Dayplan.

Share a Blueprint with one link. Anyone with the link can read it and comment; only your crew can edit it.

Stage 03 · Make

Workbench

Workbench is where the work itself lives. Drop in a render, a build or a mix and Crestora keeps every version on the lane, and tells you what changed and who changed it.

Versions

Each upload to the same file becomes its next version: v1, v2, v3 and on. Studio, Crew and Label keep every version for good (Solo keeps 30 days), and older versions are never thinned out; they simply count toward your storage. Give a version a name, such as “festival cut”, to pin it, and pinned versions show first in Review Room.

Compare

Pick any two versions and press C. Video and images open side by side with a swipe handle, audio lays both waveforms over each other, and documents and code show a line-by-line diff. A short summary at the top says what changed, such as “14 frames re-timed, grade adjusted”.

Supported files

KindFormatsCompared as
Video.mov .mp4 .mxf .webm, up to 8KSide by side, swipe, frame diff
Audio.wav .aiff .flac .mp3Overlaid waveforms, A/B playback
Images & design.png .jpg .tiff .psd .svgSwipe, onion skin, pixel diff
Documents.pdf .docx .md .txtLine-by-line redline
CodeRepositories, through GitloomCommits and line diff
3D.glb .usdz .objViewer only, for now

Single files can be up to 50 GB on Studio and Crew, and 2 GB on Solo. Review for 3D files is on the roadmap, under Now.

Stage 04 · Review

Review Room

Review Room is where feedback happens without stalling the work. Pin a note to a frame, a pixel, a second of audio or a line of code, and the person who needs to act on it sees exactly where.

Note types

Frame notes
On one frame or a range of frames in a video, with the timecode attached.
Pixel pins
A point or a drawn box on an image, a single frame or a PDF page.
Waveform notes
A moment or a stretch of audio, marked right on the waveform. New in v4.12.
Line notes
On a line, or a range of lines, in a document or a code diff.

Any note can be resolved, reopened or handed to someone as a task. Resolved notes fold away but stay on the version for good. Notes on video and audio come with Studio and Crew; images, documents and code can be reviewed on every plan.

Review links

Send anyone a review link: they open it in a browser and leave notes with just their name, with no account and no seat to pay for. A link can expire on a date, ask for a password and stamp the reviewer’s name on what they see. Revoke any link in one click from the lane’s Share menu.

On Crew, client review portals gather all of one client’s links on a single page, with your logo on it.

Approvals

When a version is right, the reviewer chooses Approve. The approval is written onto that exact version with the name and the time, so nobody has to dig through email later. On Crew, approvals keep a full audit trail and can ask for more than one person, say the director and the client.

Approved versions are what Countdown publishes, which is why a lane can’t move into Launch while a version is still waiting. When the last one is approved, the lane moves on to Launch by itself; rules of your own, like that one, are on the roadmap, under Next.

Stage 05 · Launch

Countdown

Countdown gets you from approved to out in the world on the day you said. It builds the launch checklist from your Blueprint, schedules every channel and opens a room where your crew watches it go live together.

Checklists

The checklist fills itself from the lane: the approved versions to publish, the milestones still open, the channels you picked and the copy for your Share Page. Items tick themselves off as the work gets done. Add your own, give them to someone and give them a time, like “Post the trailer, 09:00”.

Channels

A channel is anywhere the work goes out: your Share Page, a newsletter, an online store, a social scheduler, a live stream, an app-store build, a podcast feed. Channels come from the tools you’ve connected, such as Postlark, Stallfront and Clipdeck. Each gets its own time and its own copy, and the whole schedule sits on one line, so you can see what goes out when.

Studio and Crew launch to up to 14 channels at once; Solo launches to one.

Launch rooms

A launch room opens an hour before the first channel goes out. It holds the countdown, the checklist, a live feed from every channel and a chat for the crew. When the last channel is live, the room closes and its numbers become the lane’s first Afterglow recap.

Stage 06 · Afterglow

Afterglow

Afterglow shows how it landed. Views, sales, replies and reviews from every channel come together in one recap, and the ideas the launch sparked go straight back to your Sparkboard.

Recaps

Recaps arrive on day 1, day 7 and day 30 after launch, in the app and by email if you’d like them there too. Each one compares the lane with your own past launches, never with anyone else’s. Afterglow recaps come with Studio and Crew.

Metric definitions

Reach
People who saw the work at least once, across every channel, counted once each where a channel can tell.
Views
Total plays, reads or page views, counted the way each channel counts them.
Engagement
Replies, comments, shares and saves, divided by reach.
Sales
Orders and revenue from connected stores, net of refunds, in the lane’s currency.
Listen-through
For audio and video: the share of plays that reached 90 % of the running time.
Sparks returned
New sparks that came out of the launch, from replies you saved or ideas noted in the room.

Channels report at different speeds, so a recap’s numbers can still move for up to 48 hours.

Working together

Crew & roles

A crew is how lanes are shared on the Crew plan. Everyone on a lane has one of four roles, and each role sees what it needs and nothing more. Reviewers and guests are always free.

Permissions

What they can doOwnerMakerReviewerGuest
See the lane, its files and its planYesYesYesShared only
Leave notes and pinsYesYesYesYes
Approve a versionYesYesYesNo
Upload versions, edit the BlueprintYesYesNoNo
Move the lane between stagesYesYesNoNo
Schedule a launch, run the launch roomYesYesNoNo
Invite people and change rolesYesNoNoNo
Billing, export, deleting the laneYesNoNoNo

Reviewers are crew members with an account who see the whole lane. Guests arrive through a review link and see only what’s been shared with them. Only an Owner can override the approval rule to move a lane into Launch.

Working together

Integrations

More than 120 tools connect to Crestora. Their work merges into your lane: renders, builds, mixes and orders arrive as versions and stats, not as another inbox. Browse them all in the integrations directory.

Connecting a tool

  1. 01

    Open the lane, or go to Settings → Integrations to connect it for every lane.

  2. 02

    Choose the tool and the stage its work should land in.

  3. 03

    Sign in to the tool and approve what it asks for. It takes about 30 seconds.

From then on, new work arrives on its own: an export from Framewright becomes a new version in Workbench, and an order in Stallfront shows up in Afterglow.

What a tool can see

Each integration asks only for what it needs, and shows you the list before you connect: read access to one folder, say, or permission to post to one channel. A tool connected to one lane can’t see your others. Disconnect it any time from Settings → Integrations; the versions it already brought in stay on the lane.

Building something of your own? The API reference covers lanes, versions, comments, launches and webhooks.

Reference

Import & export

Your work is yours, so it comes in easily and leaves completely, on every plan.

Importing

Drop a folder on your lane list and each folder inside it becomes a lane, with its files as first versions. A CSV with one row per project brings in titles, stages, launch days and owners. Connected tools can bring their history too: Gitloom brings commits, Framewright brings past exports.

lanes.csv
title,stage,launch_on,owner
Tidepool 1.1,launch,2026-12-04,theo@tidepool.example
Small Hours ep. 49,review,2026-12-04,lena@smallhours.example
Kiln & Co. spring drop,review,,ines@kilnandco.example

Stages are written in lower case: spark, shape, make, review, launch or afterglow. Dates are YYYY-MM-DD; leave launch_on empty if there isn’t one yet.

Exporting

Export one lane or everything from Settings → Export, at any time, with no ticket and no fee. You get a .zip in open formats: every file and version as you uploaded it, notes as JSON and CSV, Blueprints as Markdown and recaps as CSV.

Inside the .zip
tidepool-1-1-export.zip
├── lane.json          title, stages, dates, crew
├── blueprint.md
├── versions/
│   ├── tidepool-ios-v12.ipa
│   └── release-notes-v4.md
├── notes.csv          every note, pin and approval
└── recaps/
    └── day-30.csv

Deleting a lane takes it out of our live systems within 24 hours and out of our backups within 30 days, so export first if you might want it back.
